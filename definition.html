Plasma: what happens when a gas gets too hot to hold its electrons

Plasma is the fourth state of matter. Keep adding heat to a gas and its atoms collide so hard that electrons are knocked off. What's left is a glowing soup of free electrons and positive ions. Drag the heat slider or tap a state to watch it happen.

Where plasma works for us

Over 99% of the visible universe is plasma, and we've learned to put it to work. Pick an example to see what the electrons, ions and heavy particles each do.

Particle counts and speeds in the chamber are illustrative, not to scale. Real plasmas can also be made with strong electric fields rather than heat alone, which is how neon signs and chip-making plasmas work.
